2D coordinate transforms

Moving a shape around turns out to be multiplication by a table of numbers. To write translation in that same form these lessons use 3×3 matrices in homogeneous coordinates, working up from a plain linear transform to rotation about an arbitrary point.

Assumed background
Coordinates and matrix multiplication.
Written for
University and adult learners
Shared convention
A point is a column vector v = [x, y, 1]^T, and matrices multiply from the left.
Order of composition
Applying M = A·B gives A(Bv): the rightmost matrix acts first.

Lessons

  1. Linear transforms

    Scaling, shear and reflection in one matrix, and why its columns are the images of the basis vectors.

  2. Translation

    The operation a 2×2 matrix cannot express, and what the third column and third row are doing.

  3. Linear transform and translation combined

    Order changes the result. Built as T·L, a point sees L first.

  4. Rotation about the origin

    Lengths and angles stay put, and the determinant stays at 1 — visible in the numbers.

  5. Rotation about an arbitrary point

    The product T(c)·R(θ)·T(−c), shown as three separate steps: move to the origin, rotate, move back.

What this material covers

  • Affine transforms only. The third row of the matrix is always [0, 0, 1]; projective transforms are not included.
  • In each lesson, lines, circles, rectangles and freehand strokes you draw are transformed by the same matrix.
  • Shapes can only be drawn with a pointer, not from the keyboard. Every parameter has both a slider and a number field.
  • No check of learning effect has been carried out. This material is a draft.

Fuller explanation

Definitions, inverse matrices, the general form for rotation about a point, and worked examples are in the written notes.
