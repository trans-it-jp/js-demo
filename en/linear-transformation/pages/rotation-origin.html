Rotation about the origin

Rotating by an angle θ about the origin. Rotation preserves lengths and angles, so the determinant stays at 1 throughout.

The question
What stays the same when you rotate?
Assumed background
Coordinates, and multiplying tables of numbers (matrices). Written for upper-secondary level and above.
Predict before you run it
At θ = 90 degrees, where do you expect the e₁ arrow to point?
Draw Click or drag on the figure to draw. Anything you draw is transformed by the same matrix.

The dashed outline is the original shape and the solid one is the result. The pale grid shows where the cells go.

Things to check

  • Compare: step θ through 30, 60 and 90 degrees and watch the determinant.
  • Explain: from the values of cos and sin, explain what the entries become at θ = 90 degrees.
  • Transfer: does θ = 180 degrees give the same matrix as scaling with sx = sy = −1? Check it.

What this page covers

  • Affine transforms only. The third row of the matrix is always [0, 0, 1]; projective transforms are not included.
  • Shapes can only be drawn with a pointer, not from the keyboard. Every parameter can also be set in a number field.
  • No check of learning effect has been carried out. This page is a draft of the material.
